**`dossier`** — adds an `## Insights` section for the interesting-but-not-load-bearing material research turns up.

- **New `## Insights` section in both templates.** Three to six items: a striking parallel, a number that reframes the scale of something, an adjacent field that solved the same problem differently, a primary source worth reading for its own sake. The gap is structural rather than an authoring slip — a brief acts as a filter, and what it filters out is not uniformly worthless, but it has nowhere to go and so gets discarded at synthesis every time. A named home changes the default from *delete* to *collect*.
- **The inversion test, to stop it becoming a dumping ground.** If removing an item would change a finding or a recommendation, it is not an insight — it is a buried finding sitting below the conclusion where the reader never reaches it, and it belongs in the body. Stated in SYNTHESIZE and enforced by review.
- **Teaser convention.** Each insight takes a stable ID (`I1`, `I2` …) and is mentioned once or twice from the body as a one-line inline pointer that links down (`([→ I3 — what the ventilation actually cost][in-I3])`). This deliberately reuses the existing `[S1][ref-S1]` citation idiom rather than inventing a second cross-reference syntax. Teasers sit at paragraph ends, never mid-argument. Without them the section lives past the point most readers stop, which makes the most memorable material in the research the least-read part of the document.
- **Insights carry the same evidence standard as findings.** Found by using the feature on a live dossier immediately after shipping it: an insight ended by asserting that a public debate had "largely ignored" a monument — a claim that was never researched, sitting in a document that grades every other claim as documented/contested/unverified. The looser register invites it, and a research-genre dossier has no assessment section where a stance could live legitimately, so an insight becomes the only place one can hide. Absence claims are the usual shape and the trailing sentence the usual site. Review now checks a third direction (stance, alongside inversion and reachability) and the fix is to move the question to *What could not be determined* rather than soften the wording.
- **Review-checklist item 12** checks all three directions — inversion (buried findings), reachability (orphans) and stance (unearned verdicts) — plus four new Common Mistakes rows.

Placed after *What the picture implies* and before the closing matter in the research template, so it enriches without interrupting the survey spine or supplying headings above it.
